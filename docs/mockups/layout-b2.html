Option B, with a sidebar in each pane

You were right — this is the familiar shape, and a sidebar does something a dropdown can't: you can drag files straight onto a favourite to send them there without navigating either pane. Both sidebars collapse independently with ⌘1 / ⌘2.

Everything from before, plus a Finder-style sidebar per pane. The phone sidebar carries the device and its storage meter; the Mac sidebar mirrors Finder's favourites. Note the dashed drop target — that's a drag in progress onto Camera.

Drag onto a favourite. The dashed outline on Camera is what you'd see mid-drag. Drop there and the files go to /sdcard/DCIM/Camera — the right-hand pane never has to leave /sdcard/Download. This works in both directions and is the single best reason to have sidebars at all.

B2 · alternativeSidebars, no centre column — direction in the toolbar

Two sidebars plus a 74px action column is a lot of vertical dividers. This version reclaims those pixels by moving the four actions into the toolbar, where the labels still name their direction explicitly — so there's still no "which pane has focus?" rule to learn.

B1 or B2? The trade is 74 pixels against how loudly the app announces which way a move is going. I still lean B1 — the centre column sits between the two panes, which is exactly where your eye already is when you're deciding what goes where — but B2 is the better fit if you mostly work in a smaller window, and the explicit labels keep it safe.
