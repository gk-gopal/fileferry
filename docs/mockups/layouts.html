Conduit — three dual-pane layouts

Same underlying app in all three: a Mac folder on one side, a phone folder on the other, copy and move in both directions, no history kept. What differs is how you express direction — and since a move deletes the original, direction being unambiguous is a safety property, not just a convenience.

Which of these feels like "copying or moving between 2 folders" to you?

Option A

Side by side, actions in the toolbar

The familiar shape — Transmit, ForkLift, OpenMTP. Direction comes from which pane has focus: the toolbar acts from the focused pane to the other one. Maximum room for files.

Pros

  • Widest file panes — most rows and longest names visible
  • Instantly familiar to anyone who has used an FTP client
  • Drag between panes still works for people who prefer it

Cons

  • Direction depends on focus, which is a subtle visual cue
  • "Move →" with the wrong pane focused deletes the wrong original
  • Toolbar labels must flip as focus changes, or they lie
Option B · recommended

Side by side with a centre action column

Four explicit buttons sit between the panes. Each one names its own direction, so there is no focused-pane rule to learn and no way to misread which way a move is about to go. Costs about 74px of width.

Pros

  • Direction is stated on the button, never inferred — the safety win
  • Copy and move are visibly different actions, move tinted red
  • Nothing to discover; a first-time user cannot get it wrong

Cons

  • Slightly utilitarian look — less like a modern Mac app
  • Four buttons is more chrome than a single toolbar pair
Option C

Stacked — Mac above, phone below

Same two folders, rotated. Each pane gets the window's full width, so long filenames and extra columns fit comfortably. The trade is vertical: each list shows roughly half as many rows.

Pros

  • Full-width lists — room for Kind, Modified, long names
  • "Down to phone / up to Mac" is a clear spatial metaphor
  • Works well on a narrow window

Cons

  • Halves visible rows — worst fit for a 13" laptop, which is wide and short
  • Comparing two folders means looking up and down, not across
  • Scrolling a long folder pushes the other pane out of mind

Why I lean B. Everything else here is taste, but move deletes the original once the copy verifies, and A's direction rule is "whichever pane you last clicked." That's a fine rule for copying and a bad one for deleting. B spends 74 pixels to make the direction impossible to misread. If you'd rather have the pixels, A plus a confirmation sheet on every move gets to a similar place — just with a dialog you'll learn to dismiss without reading.

Not shown: the single-pane design I suggested earlier, where only the phone appears and the Mac side is a Finder window you drag to. Your "like copying or moving between 2 folders" rules it out — it makes the Mac folder invisible and move awkward.
